How to Book Cheap Flights in 2026: 9 Tactics That Work (Even When Prices Are High)

By Amara Nwosu 13 min read Travel Tips
Disclosure: Some links in this guide may earn LitExplore a small commission at no extra cost to you. That never changes what we recommend, and we only link to services we think are useful.

Two people sitting side by side on the same flight can have paid wildly different fares. The gap can be hundreds of dollars, and luck usually has little to do with it.

Airline prices are set by software that adjusts fares constantly based on demand, how fast seats are selling, what competitors charge and years of booking data. Most people treat it as a mystery and book whenever they're ready. People who regularly pay less know just enough about how it works to use it to their advantage.

I've been testing flight booking tactics for years, so let me say this first: no single trick works every time. The idea that searching on a Tuesday gets you cheaper fares is a myth. What works is combining a few good habits, using the right tools and knowing when being flexible saves real money.

These nine make the biggest difference.

9 Tactics That Work

1

Use Google Flights as your price intelligence tool, not just a search engine

Most people use Google Flights like a booking site: type in dates, pick the cheapest, done. That misses its best features. The most useful is the date grid (after entering your route, open the date picker and choose the date grid view). It shows prices for a range of departure and return dates side by side, so you can spot the cheapest combination at a glance instead of searching date by date. A Monday departure that costs $750 might sit next to a Wednesday departure at $480. The date grid makes this visible in 30 seconds.

The second is the Explore map. Enter where you're leaving from and your dates, leave the destination blank, and Google Flights fills a world map with the cheapest fares to hundreds of places. Start here when you know when you can travel but not where.

2

Set fare alerts immediately, before you're ready to book

This takes almost no effort and often saves the most. On Google Flights, search your route, then click the toggle to track prices. On Skyscanner, set a price alert from the search results page. Both will email you when the price changes noticeably.

Set alerts as soon as a trip idea forms, not once you've decided. On many routes, fares dip for a day or two during sales and then climb back. If you wait until your visa and hotel are sorted before looking at flights, you'll often miss those dips. Set the alert first and plan everything else alongside it. If you need a visa, choose a flexible or refundable fare until it's approved.

3

Book long-haul flights roughly 2 to 5 months ahead

There's no magic number, but for most international trips the best prices tend to show up somewhere between about 2 and 5 months before departure. Book a year out and you often pay a high opening fare. Book in the last few weeks and you pay for seats that are running out. Google Flights shows whether today's price is low, typical or high for your route, which helps you decide.

For peak times such as Christmas, summer school holidays, Carnival or Songkran, book at the earlier end, because cheap seats go quickly. For shoulder and low season, two to four months ahead usually works well. Leaving an international booking until the last month or so nearly always costs more.

4

Be flexible on the week, not just the day

Midweek flights are sometimes a little cheaper, but it isn't a reliable rule. What changes prices far more is which week you fly. Shifting your trip one week earlier or later can easily save 20 to 35% on the same route. Use Skyscanner's whole-month view or Google Flights' date grid to find the cheapest week in your travel window, then plan the rest of the trip around it.

From West Africa, some of the cheapest periods tend to be early September, late January to February, and late May to early June, when you avoid the big holiday rushes at both ends of the trip.

5

Check other airports at both ends

Flying into a secondary airport and taking a train or bus can be much cheaper. Think London Stansted instead of Heathrow with the Stansted Express into town, Paris Beauvais instead of Charles de Gaulle, or Milan Bergamo instead of Malpensa. Add the cost and time of getting into the city before you decide.

Where you fly from matters too. Sometimes it's cheaper to take a domestic flight to a bigger hub and start your international trip there, though leave plenty of time between the two separate bookings. In Nigeria, compare Lagos and Abuja on the same long-haul route, because prices can differ a lot depending on the airline.

6

Compare the total price, not the headline fare

A cheap base fare can end up costing more once you add checked bags, seat selection and card fees. Budget airlines in particular often include only a small under-seat bag. Before you compare two options, price them the way you'll actually travel: with your bags, any seats you need, and the payment method you'll use.

On long trips from Nigeria, check whether the fare includes one or two checked bags and how heavy they can be. A fare that's $60 cheaper but includes one bag instead of two can cost you far more at the check-in desk. It takes two minutes and can change which flight is really the cheapest.

7

Consider two one-way tickets instead of a round-trip

This is most useful when you fly into one city and home from another, often called an open-jaw or multi-city trip. Budget airlines price each direction separately, and mixing airlines can sometimes beat a round trip. On long-haul routes with full-service airlines, though, one-way tickets can be expensive, so price it both ways. For example, Lagos to Bangkok, Bangkok to Nairobi and Nairobi back to Lagos on different airlines can occasionally cost less than a Lagos-Bangkok round trip plus a separate side trip.

The tools that help here are Skyscanner (search multi-city directly) and Google Flights' multi-city mode. The catch is that separate tickets don't protect each other. If one flight is delayed or cancelled, the next airline doesn't have to help you, so leave generous gaps between flights.

8

Sign up for fare deal alerts, but choose carefully

Deal services like the Going app (formerly Scott's Cheap Flights) hunt for mistake fares and flash sales, when an airline prices a route far lower than intended or runs a short sale. A round trip from the US to Europe that normally costs $900 might show up for $400. These deals usually last a day or two before they disappear.

The catch: you need to be able to book quickly when an alert comes in. If you need two weeks to decide, these alerts will just frustrate you. If you can commit when the price is right, they can be worth paying for. Most of their deals are for departures from the US, UK and Europe, so check coverage for your home airport first. Going's free tier covers some deals; the paid tier ($49/year) gets you more routes and premium economy and business class deals.

9

Book directly with the airline after finding the best price

Use comparison tools (Google Flights, Skyscanner, Kayak) to find the best price, then go directly to the airline's website to complete the booking. Airlines usually charge the same or less on their own websites, and booking direct makes life much easier if something goes wrong. Changes, cancellations, seat upgrades and name corrections are all quicker when you deal with the airline rather than going through an online travel agency (OTA) in the middle.

Now and then an OTA really is cheaper, especially for flight and hotel packages. If it's about 10% cheaper or more, the saving may be worth the extra hassle. If the gap is smaller, book with the airline. Stick to well-known OTAs with good reviews.

The Tools I Use

Google Flights
Best for: Date flexibility, price tracking, route exploration. Use the date grid and Explore map. Free, no booking fees. Best starting point for most searches.
Skyscanner
Best for: Widest coverage including budget airlines, "Everywhere" destination search, month-view calendar. Often finds budget carriers Google misses.
Going (app)
Best for: Mistake fares and flash deals. The alerts come to you, so it suits flexible travelers who can book within a day.
Kayak
Best for: Price forecasts (shows whether current prices are "typical," "low," or "high" for the route). Good cross-check against Google Flights.
Airline websites direct
Best for: Final booking after finding the price on a comparison tool. Loyalty program miles, easier changes, direct support line access.

Specific Notes for Travelers From Nigeria and West Africa

Most flight guides are written for people flying from the US or UK. From West Africa, things look quite different.

For long-haul routes to Europe, Asia, and the Americas from Lagos or Abuja, the primary hubs to compare are: Turkish Airlines via Istanbul, Emirates via Dubai, Qatar Airways via Doha, Ethiopian Airlines via Addis Ababa, and Air France/KLM via Paris/Amsterdam. Each prices differently by season and route. Turkish Airlines often has good fares to Southeast Asia and a huge network. Ethiopian is often the best value for East Africa and some European routes. For North America, Delta and United fly nonstop from Lagos, and their fares can be competitive outside peak periods.

For departures from Lagos, booking about 10 to 14 weeks ahead works well for most international routes, and 16 to 20 weeks ahead for December. Price alerts are especially useful here, because fares out of Nigeria can swing sharply with exchange rates and demand.

What Doesn't Work in 2026

Three myths worth retiring:

"Tuesday is always cheapest." Fares change with demand, not with the day you happen to search. Prices can fall on any day. When you fly, and how far ahead you book, matter far more.

"Searching in incognito mode gets you a lower price." Studies that have tested this haven't found airlines raising fares because of your cookies. If a price goes up after a few searches, it's usually because seats sold or a sale ended. Private browsing won't hurt, but don't expect it to save you money.

"Booking as early as possible is always best." As mentioned above, the best fares for international trips usually show up a few months before departure. Booking 11 months out often means paying a high opening price for seats that later go on sale.

If you remember one thing

Set a fare alert on Google Flights the day a trip idea forms, use Skyscanner's month view to find the cheapest week, compare total prices including bags, and book directly with the airline once the price looks right. Those habits together will save you more than any single clever trick.
