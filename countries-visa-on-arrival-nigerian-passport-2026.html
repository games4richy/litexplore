Countries Nigerian Passport Holders Can Visit Without a Visa in 2026: Full List With Conditions

By Amara Nwosu11 min readVisa and Immigration

Few things derail a trip faster than finding out at the check-in desk that you needed a visa. Airline staff in Lagos and Abuja check your documents before you board, and if your paperwork is wrong, you don't fly. For Nigerian passport holders, knowing exactly where you can go without applying in advance saves money, stress and a wasted ticket.

The Nigerian passport sits around 88th on the Henley Passport Index in 2026, with visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to roughly 44 destinations. That's far fewer than a US, UK or EU passport gets, and many of the best-known trips still need a visa. But the list includes most of West Africa, several East African countries, a handful of Caribbean and Pacific islands, and the Maldives. Add countries with quick online eVisas and the options widen a lot.

Visa rules change often, and online lists copy each other's mistakes. Use this guide to plan, then confirm the rules for your exact trip on the destination's official immigration website before you pay for flights.

Visa-Free Destinations (No Application Needed)

ECOWAS countries
Under the ECOWAS free movement protocol, Nigerians can visit Benin, Burkina Faso, Cape Verde, Côte d'Ivoire, The Gambia, Ghana, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Liberia, Mali, Niger, Senegal, Sierra Leone and Togo without a visa, generally for up to 90 days. Carry your international passport rather than relying on an ECOWAS travel certificate or ID card, and bring your yellow fever vaccination card, which border officials often ask for. Ghana is the easiest first trip: short flights, no language barrier, and plenty to see from Accra to Cape Coast.
Kenya
In 2025 Kenya dropped its Electronic Travel Authorisation requirement for most African nationals. Nigerians can now visit without a visa or eTA for up to 60 days. Nairobi, the Maasai Mara, Diani Beach and Mount Kenya make it one of the most rewarding safari and beach trips on the continent.
Cameroon and Chad
Both are visa-free for Nigerians. Check your government's travel advisory first, because some border regions in both countries have serious security problems.
Barbados
Visa-free for up to six months, one of the most generous allowances a Nigerian passport gets anywhere. Bring a return ticket, proof of accommodation and bank statements, as immigration officers may ask. There are no direct flights, so you will usually connect through London, New York or Miami, and those routes need a UK or US transit visa (see the transit section below).
Dominica and St Kitts and Nevis
Dominica allows visa-free stays of up to six months and St Kitts and Nevis up to three months. Both are small, quiet islands with rainforest, hiking and uncrowded beaches. Getting there usually means connecting through a hub that needs a transit visa, so plan your route carefully.
Haiti
Technically visa-free for up to three months. In practice, most governments currently advise against all travel to Haiti because of the security situation, so this is not a realistic holiday option right now.
Pacific islands: Fiji, Vanuatu, Micronesia, Samoa
Fiji allows visa-free visits of up to four months, Vanuatu and Micronesia 30 days, and Samoa issues a free entry permit on arrival for 60 days. They are beautiful but expensive to reach from Nigeria, and most routes connect through Australia, New Zealand or the US, which all require visas even to transit. Check your routing before you get excited.

Visa on Arrival

Maldives
Every nationality gets a free 30-day visa on arrival, provided you have a confirmed hotel booking, a return ticket and enough money for your stay. You must also complete the online IMUGA traveller declaration before arrival. Overwater villas can cost $300 to $1,000 a night, but guesthouses on local islands start at around $50 to $120 and give you the same turquoise water.
Rwanda
Nigerians can get a 30-day visa on arrival at Kigali airport, and Rwanda has waived the fee for African Union citizens in recent years. Confirm the current rules on Rwanda's immigration website before you go. Kigali is clean, safe and well organized. Gorilla trekking in Volcanoes National Park is unforgettable, but the permit costs $1,500.
Mauritius
Nigerians are listed as eligible for a short visa on arrival (about two weeks) for tourism. Officers will want to see a return ticket, a hotel booking and proof of funds. Rules for Nigerian travelers to Mauritius have been tightened before, so check with the Mauritius Passport and Immigration Office before booking.
Cambodia, Ethiopia, Uganda, Mozambique, Madagascar and Comoros
All of these offer a visa on arrival or an online eVisa to Nigerians. Where you have the choice, apply online. It is usually quicker at the airport and you know you're approved before you fly. Cambodia's 30-day eVisa covers Angkor Wat, and Ethiopia's eVisa lets Ethiopian Airlines passengers turn a layover in Addis Ababa into a short stopover.

Quick eVisas Worth Knowing About

These countries are not visa-free, but you apply online, usually within days, with no embassy visit. They are often easier than people assume.

Vietnam
Vietnam's eVisa is open to every nationality. A 90-day single-entry visa costs $25 and usually takes 3 to 5 working days. Apply only on the official portal at evisa.gov.vn, because copycat sites charge far more.
Malaysia
Nigerians need a Malaysian eVisa, applied for online before travel. You also need to complete the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card within three days of arrival. Kuala Lumpur and Penang are affordable and have some of Asia's best street food.
Thailand
Nigerians apply through Thailand's official eVisa website. Expect to upload bank statements, flight and hotel bookings, and proof of employment. Every traveler also has to fill in the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card before landing.
Philippines
A visa is required. Nigerian applicants can apply online through the Philippines eVisa system or through the Philippine Embassy in Abuja, depending on current arrangements, so check the embassy's website first. Palawan and Siargao are worth the paperwork.
Qatar
Nigerians apply online for a tourist eVisa through the Hayya platform. Doha makes a good stopover of two or three days on the way to Asia with Qatar Airways.
Trinidad and Tobago and Colombia
Both issue eVisas to Nigerians. Trinidad's Carnival season in February or March is the time to go if you can plan ahead. In Colombia, Medellín and Cartagena reward the effort.

Countries Where a US, UK or Schengen Visa Helps

Several countries that normally require Nigerians to apply at an embassy make an exception if you already hold a valid visa or residence permit from certain Western countries.

Georgia
Nigerians normally need a visa, which you can apply for online. However, if you hold a valid visa or residence permit from the US, UK, Schengen Area or several other countries, you can usually visit Georgia visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Tbilisi is cheap, safe and full of good food and wine.
Turkey
Nigerians without other visas must apply for a sticker visa through the Turkish embassy or its visa center. If you hold a valid Schengen, US, UK or Irish visa or residence permit, you may qualify for Turkey's conditional eVisa at evisa.gov.tr, which is much faster. Always check that the conditions match your situation before you apply.
Mexico
Travelers with a valid US, UK, Canadian, Japanese or Schengen visa can usually enter Mexico as tourists without a Mexican visa. Without one, you need to apply at a Mexican embassy.
Not visa-free, despite what some lists say

Older articles still claim Nigerians can visit Malaysia, Singapore, the Philippines, Georgia, Jamaica, Turkey or Tanzania without a visa. In 2026 all of these need a visa or eVisa arranged in advance, unless you qualify for one of the exceptions above. Airlines will refuse to board you without the right document.

Practical Tips for Nigerian Passport Holders

Build your travel history on purpose. Stamps from trips you took legally make later applications to the UK, US, Canada and Schengen countries easier. Start with visa-free and eVisa destinations such as Ghana, Kenya, Rwanda, Vietnam and Malaysia. A passport that shows you travel and come home on time carries real weight with visa officers.

Check the official source, then check again. Rules for Nigerian travelers have changed in both directions over the years. Use the destination's official immigration or embassy website, not blogs or agents, and look again a week before you fly. If anything is unclear, email or call the embassy.

Check transit rules for every stopover. This catches many Nigerian travelers out. The UK requires Nigerians to hold a Direct Airside Transit Visa even when changing planes without leaving the airport, and Schengen countries such as France, Germany and the Netherlands require an Airport Transit Visa. The US requires a full visa even to transit. Connections through Dubai, Doha, Istanbul and Addis Ababa generally don't need a transit visa if you stay airside and have a confirmed onward flight on the same booking, but always confirm with your airline.

Carry proof, even when no visa is needed. Visa-free doesn't mean no questions. Keep your return ticket, hotel booking, recent bank statement and yellow fever card in your hand luggage. Officers at the airport, and airline staff in Nigeria, may ask to see them.

Good first trips from Nigeria with no visa hassle

Ghana for an easy long weekend in Accra and Cape Coast. Kenya for safari and the coast, visa-free for 60 days. Rwanda for gorillas, green hills and a calm capital. Senegal for Dakar's music and food scene. The Maldives if you're saving up for something special. And if you're happy to fill in an online form, Vietnam and Malaysia open up Southeast Asia at very low cost.

Important: Visa rules, processing times, and entry requirements change frequently. Always verify current requirements directly with the official embassy or consulate of your destination country before applying.
