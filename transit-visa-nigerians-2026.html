Transit Visas for Nigerians 2026: Which Airports You Can Connect Through Without One

By Amara NwosuSeptember 13, 20267 min readVisa and Immigration

It happens at check-in counters in Lagos and Abuja more often than it should. A traveler has a visa for their final destination, a confirmed ticket and packed bags, and the airline refuses to board them. The reason: their connection is in a country that requires Nigerians to hold a transit visa, even to change planes.

Transit rules are easy to overlook because you never plan to leave the airport. But for Nigerian passport holders, a few of the world's busiest hubs require a visa just to connect, while others let you pass through freely. Choosing the right connection can save you a visa fee, weeks of paperwork or a wasted ticket. This guide explains how transit works and which major airports Nigerians can and can't connect through without a visa in 2026.

Always confirm before you book

Transit rules change, and exemptions depend on the exact documents you hold. Before paying for a ticket, check with your airline, which uses the same international database border agencies rely on, and check the official government website of every country you'll connect in.

Airside vs Landside: The Key Idea

Airside transit means you stay in the international transit area and walk from one gate to another without passing through immigration. Many countries don't require a visa for this.

Landside transit means you pass through immigration, even briefly, for example to collect bags, change terminals or check in again with a different airline. Once you pass immigration, you usually need a visa to enter that country.

Whether your connection stays airside depends on three things:

  • One ticket or separate tickets. On a single booking, your bags are usually checked through to your final destination. With separate tickets, you often have to collect your bags and check in again, which means passing immigration.
  • The airport layout. Some airports make you pass through border control to change terminals.
  • The length of your layover. Very long or overnight layovers may require you to leave the transit area, and some countries limit how long you can stay airside.

Where Nigerians Usually Need a Transit Visa

United Kingdom (London Heathrow, Gatwick, Manchester)

Transit visa usually required
Nigerians need a Direct Airside Transit Visa (DATV) to change planes in the UK, even without leaving the airport, or a Visitor in Transit visa if they'll pass through UK border control. There are exemptions. You usually don't need a DATV if you hold a valid visa or residence permit for the United States, Canada, Australia or New Zealand, a valid residence permit or long-stay visa from an EEA country or Switzerland, or, in some cases, a valid Irish biometric visa. The exact conditions are on the UK government's transit visa checker, so use it before you book.

Schengen Area (Paris, Amsterdam, Frankfurt, Brussels, Madrid and others)

Transit visa usually required
Nigeria is on the EU's list of countries whose citizens need an Airport Transit Visa (ATV) to pass through the international transit area of Schengen airports. You're generally exempt if you hold a valid visa or residence permit issued by a Schengen or EEA country, or a valid visa for the United States, Canada or Japan, among other exemptions. Be especially careful at large airports such as Paris Charles de Gaulle, where changing terminals can take you through border control. If that happens, an ATV isn't enough and you'd need a Schengen visa.

Ireland (Dublin)

Transit visa required
Ireland requires Nigerian citizens to hold an Irish transit visa to pass through an Irish airport, unless an exemption applies. Check Ireland's immigration website before booking a connection in Dublin.

United States

Full visa required
US airports don't have international transit areas where you can stay without entering the country. Every connecting passenger passes through US immigration, so Nigerians need a US visa just to change planes. With US visa issuance for Nigerians heavily restricted since January 2026, connecting through the US is not a realistic option for most Nigerian travelers right now.

Canada (Toronto, Montreal, Vancouver)

Transit visa required
Nigerians need a Canadian transit visa to connect through a Canadian airport, even for a short layover. Nigeria isn't part of Canada's Transit Without Visa program. The transit visa itself has no fee, but you still have to apply in advance and give biometrics, so allow plenty of time.

Hubs Where Nigerians Can Usually Connect Without a Visa

At these airports, Nigerians can generally change planes without a visa, provided they stay airside, have a confirmed onward flight on the same booking and hold any visa needed for their final destination.

Dubai (DXB)

Airside transit usually fine
You can connect airside in Dubai without a UAE visa, typically for layovers of up to 24 hours. If you want to leave the airport, you'll need a UAE visa, which some airlines can arrange for longer layovers. Arrange it before you travel, not at the transfer desk.

Doha (DOH)

Airside transit usually fine
Hamad International Airport has a large, comfortable transit area, and Nigerians can connect airside without a visa. Leaving the airport requires a Qatari visa, applied for in advance.

Istanbul (IST)

Airside transit usually fine
Istanbul Airport is one of the busiest connecting points for flights from Nigeria to Europe, Asia and the Americas. If you stay in the international transit area, you don't need a Turkish visa. To leave the airport, including on free city tours offered to some transit passengers, you'd need a Turkish visa.

Addis Ababa (ADD)

Airside transit usually fine
Ethiopian Airlines' hub is one of the most popular connection points for Nigerians heading to Asia, the Middle East and East Africa. Airside transit doesn't require a visa. If you'd like to see Addis Ababa on a longer layover, Ethiopia offers eVisas and visas on arrival for many nationalities, including Nigerians, and Ethiopian Airlines runs a stopover program.

Casablanca (CMN)

Usually fine on one ticket
Royal Air Maroc passengers on a single booking can generally connect airside in Casablanca without a Moroccan visa. Leaving the airport, including for airline transit hotels on long layovers, requires permission to enter Morocco, so check your options before booking a long connection.

Johannesburg (JNB)

Airside only
You can usually connect airside at OR Tambo International without a visa. To leave the airport, or if your connection forces you through immigration, you'll need a South African visa. See our South Africa visa guide for Nigerians.

Nairobi (NBO)

No visa needed
Nigerians can visit Kenya without a visa or eTA for up to 60 days, so connecting in Nairobi, or even stopping over, is straightforward. See our Kenya travel guide.

Choosing the Right Connection

A few examples of how this plays out in real bookings:

  • Lagos to Toronto on a Canadian visa. A connection in London usually works, because a valid Canadian visa exempts you from the UK DATV. A connection in Paris or Amsterdam usually works too, because a valid Canadian visa also exempts you from the Schengen ATV. Connecting in the US doesn't work without a US visa.
  • Lagos to Dubai, Bangkok or Kuala Lumpur. Gulf, Istanbul and Addis Ababa connections are the easiest, since they don't need a transit visa if you stay airside.
  • Lagos to London on a UK visa. Fly nonstop, or connect through Istanbul, Casablanca, Addis Ababa or the Gulf. A UK visa isn't on the Schengen exemption list, so connecting in Paris, Amsterdam or Frankfurt usually means applying for an ATV first.
  • Lagos to Johannesburg or Nairobi. Nonstop is simplest. With a stop, Addis Ababa and Nairobi are easy options for Nigerians.

Mistakes That Get Travelers Stuck

🎫

Booking separate tickets through a visa-required hub

Two cheap tickets on different airlines can force you to collect bags and check in again, which means entering the country. If you don't have a visa for that country, you're stuck. Book connections on a single ticket wherever possible.

🔄

Forgetting about terminal changes

At some large airports, moving between terminals means passing through passport control. Check how your connection works at that specific airport before booking.

⏳

Very short or very long layovers

A tight connection risks a missed flight. If you're rebooked onto a flight the next day, you may need to leave the transit area for a hotel, which requires a visa at some airports. Overnight layovers carry the same risk.

📄

Relying on an exemption without the right document

Exemptions usually need a valid, current visa or residence permit, often physically in your passport or on a card. An expired visa, or one for a different country than the rule lists, doesn't count. Carry the document with you, not just a photo of it.

Before you pay for any ticket with a connection

Check every country your flights stop in, not just your destination. Confirm the rules with the airline and on each country's official website. Book the whole trip on one ticket, avoid connecting in the US or Canada unless you hold visas for them, and bring any visa or residence permit that gives you an exemption. When in doubt, choose a hub such as Istanbul, Addis Ababa, Doha or Dubai, where Nigerians can usually connect airside without a visa.

Important: Visa rules, processing times, and entry requirements change frequently. Always verify current requirements directly with the official embassy or consulate of your destination country before applying.
