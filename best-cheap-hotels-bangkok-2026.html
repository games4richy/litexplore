Best Cheap Hotels in Bangkok 2026: Neighborhood Guide, Budget Picks, and What to Expect at Each Price Point

By Amara Nwosu14 min readHotels
Disclosure: Some links in this guide may earn LitExplore a small commission at no extra cost to you. That never changes what we recommend, and we only link to services we think are useful.

The best advice I can give on Bangkok hotels is about location more than price. Two people can pay the same nightly rate and have completely different trips, one hopping on the Skytrain in minutes and the other stuck in taxis for an hour each way.

Rooms here are great value at every level. A clean, air-conditioned private room with a decent bathroom costs $15 to $35 a night. A smart mid-range hotel with a pool costs $45 to $80. For $80 to $130 you can stay somewhere with real style, good service and a rooftop. Few big cities come close. Even so, the neighborhood you pick will shape your stay as much as the room itself.

Below are the main areas for budget and mid-range travelers, what each price level gets you, and some well-reviewed places to start your search in 2026.

Start With the BTS and MRT Map

Bangkok traffic is brutal. Four kilometers by road can take 45 minutes at rush hour. A hotel that looks central on the map can cost you an extra hour every day if you need a taxi just to reach a station. The BTS Skytrain (elevated) and the MRT (mostly underground) cover most of the areas visitors care about and run from about 6 AM to midnight. A single BTS trip costs roughly 17 to 65 baht ($0.50 to $1.90). From On Nut to Siam takes around 20 minutes.

My rule is simple. Stay no more than a 10-minute walk from a BTS or MRT station. That matters more than the neighborhood name, the star rating or the rooftop pool. A $20 guesthouse two minutes from a station beats a $35 one where every outing starts with a taxi.

Now for the neighborhoods.

Bangkok Neighborhood Guide for Budget Travelers

Banglamphu and Khao San Road

Best for

Khao San Road has been Southeast Asia's best-known backpacker strip for more than 30 years, and it still has the cheapest beds in Bangkok. Dorms go for $5 to $8 a night and guesthouse private rooms for $15 to $25. At those prices the rooms are fine: clean enough, air-conditioned, with lockers in the dorms. What you are paying for is the price, the social atmosphere, and proximity to the Grand Palace and Wat Pho, which are walking distance.

Keep in mind that Khao San Road is its own little world, not a slice of everyday Bangkok. Apart from the rooms, almost everything costs more than elsewhere in the city. Food on Khao San Road costs two to four times what identical food costs 10 minutes' walk away. The bars and restaurants are aimed at foreigners. The tuk-tuk and taxi drivers at the top of the street are among the most aggressive and overpriced in the city.

The practical problem is transport. There is no BTS here. To reach Siam, Sukhumvit or Silom you take a taxi to a station (15 to 20 minutes and 50 to 80 baht if traffic is light) or a boat on the Chao Phraya. You can live with it, but it adds time to every day. The wider area of Banglamphu and Phra Nakhon is well worth exploring beyond the main strip: traditional communities, excellent local food on the surrounding streets, and close proximity to the Grand Palace and Temple of the Dawn (Wat Arun). If you want the cheapest beds in town and can live with that, Banglamphu works. If fast transport matters to you, look elsewhere.

D&D Inn
From $18/night private room, from $8 dorm

A long-standing Khao San favorite with steady reviews. The big draw is the rooftop pool and bar, which you would pay far more for in Sukhumvit. Rooms are clean and the aircon works. Few guesthouses at this price have a pool at all.

New Siam Riverside
From $22/night private room

On the river rather than Khao San itself, meaning slightly quieter and with a pleasant riverside setting. River views from some rooms. Good common areas and a well-run front desk. Being on the river means you can hop on a boat to the riverside temples and piers.

Baan Chantra
From $25/night private room

A traditional Thai shophouse conversion a few streets from Khao San, in a quieter pocket of the neighborhood. Fewer travelers, more local character. Rooftop terrace with city views. The owners are friendly and give good local tips, not just the standard tourist-strip suggestions.

Sukhumvit (Nana to Asoke, BTS/MRT)

Best for

The Sukhumvit corridor follows the BTS line east from Nana through Asoke, Phrom Phong and Thong Lor. It has the widest choice of rooms at every price, easy food (street stalls alongside restaurants aimed at expats and tourists), and quick train links to Chatuchak weekend market and the Airport Rail Link. For the Grand Palace you will need a taxi or the MRT plus a short walk.

Budget rooms in central Sukhumvit (Soi 1 to 20) cost a bit more than on Khao San Road, but not much. A private room in a decent guesthouse on Soi 11 or Soi 15 runs $25 to $40 a night. Dorm beds in the better hostels in this area run $12 to $18. For the extra few dollars you get a station on your doorstep, a far wider choice of food, and a neighborhood where people live and work, not just party.

For cheap eats around Asoke, the Pier 21 food court inside Terminal 21 mall is hard to beat, with most dishes well under 100 baht. Thong Lor and Ekkamai, a few stops further out, are where Bangkok's young professionals go for cafes and independent restaurants. Rooms there tend to be pricier, but the food scene is some of the best in the city.

Jellybean The Blocks Hostel (Sukhumvit Soi 10)
From about $10/dorm

A budget hostel on a quiet side street off lower Sukhumvit, a short walk from BTS Nana and Asoke. Air-conditioned rooms, free WiFi and a snack bar. A good pick if you want a cheap bed close to the Skytrain without Khao San's noise.

Oneday Hostel (Sukhumvit Soi 26)
From about $12/dorm

A stylish hostel near BTS Phrom Phong with a cafe and coworking space on the ground floor. It draws remote workers and longer-stay guests as much as backpackers, so it's calmer than a party hostel. Emporium and EmQuartier malls, and plenty of cheap noodle shops, are within walking distance.

On Nut and Bearing (BTS, South Sukhumvit)

On Nut has been my go-to tip for budget travelers for years. It is on the BTS Sukhumvit line, far enough out that rooms cost 30 to 40% less than similar places in central Sukhumvit, yet only about 20 minutes by Skytrain from Siam. The streets around the station are full of cheap local food: pad kra pao for around 50 baht ($1.40), fruit stalls, roast duck rice, som tam carts and iced coffee stands. You'll eat better than on Khao San Road and pay about half as much.

As more budget travelers have discovered On Nut, the choice of places to stay has grown. Clean, air-conditioned private rooms with their own bathroom go for $20 to $30 a night, which is hard to find closer in. Small hotels and serviced apartments charge $40 to $70 for a very comfortable room, often with a pool. Serviced apartments are especially good value if you are staying a week or more.

Most places here are small and independent, and their quality varies a lot. On Agoda or Google Maps, search around BTS On Nut, set a walking-distance filter, and only look at places with plenty of recent reviews that mention cleanliness and noise.

Silom is Bangkok's financial and business district, which means it empties out on weekends when hotel rates drop significantly. A mid-range business hotel in Silom that charges $90 to $120 on Tuesday and Wednesday regularly drops to $55 to $70 on Friday and Saturday nights when demand falls. If you are traveling on a weekend, Silom offers very good value in a well-connected, less touristy neighborhood. The street food on Silom Soi 20 and the lanes around it is excellent and aimed almost entirely at local office workers.

For private rooms, several business hotels here drop below $50 on the booking sites during quiet periods. From BTS Saphan Taksin you can switch straight onto the river boats to Chinatown and the Old City.

Lub d Bangkok Silom
From about $14/dorm

The original Lub d, on Decho Road a short walk from BTS Chong Nonsi. It's a sociable, well-run hostel with female-only dorms and private rooms as well as shared ones. A strong choice for backpackers who want company and a station close by.

iSanook Bangkok (Bang Rak)
From about $30/night private room

Small hotel on Soi Songphra, off Siphraya Road, between Hua Lamphong and River City. You can walk to the river, and Chinatown and Hua Lamphong MRT station are close by. Rooms are simple and clean, and the lanes around it are good for cheap local food.

Ozone Hotel @ Samyan
From about $35/night private room

A no-frills hotel near Chulalongkorn University and MRT Sam Yan, one stop from Silom. Rooms have soundproof windows and a desk, and Siam's malls are a short ride away. Good if you want a proper hotel room without paying Silom prices.

What Your Money Gets You in Bangkok in 2026

Prices and quality in Bangkok climb in fairly clear steps. Knowing what each step gets you helps you avoid a cheap room that disappoints, or paying mid-range prices when a budget room would have done.

Under $12/night

Hostel Dorm

Shared room, 4 to 12 beds. Expect a mattress, pillow, locker (sometimes), shared bathroom, and air conditioning. WiFi quality varies. At this price, location and cleanliness matter most. Search recent reviews for "bed bugs" and read what people say about the bathrooms.

$15 to $28/night

Budget Private Room

Private room, shared or private bathroom. Air conditioning standard. WiFi usually adequate. Expect thin walls and basic furniture. It will do the job but won't be cozy. Some places near the top of this range are very good. Read reviews carefully: a $20/night room can be excellent or barely tolerable depending on the property.

$28 to $55/night

Mid-Budget Private Room

Private room with ensuite bathroom. Air conditioning and reliable WiFi. Often a small lobby or common area. Some properties in this range have pools. Even the weaker places at this price are decent: clean rooms, working bathrooms, and air conditioning that keeps up with the heat. From here up, Bangkok is excellent value by international standards.

$55 to $100/night

Comfortable Boutique or Business Hotel

Well-designed rooms, a pool, a restaurant or cafe, fast WiFi and helpful front desk staff. This is Bangkok's sweet spot. For $70 to $90 you can stay in a lovely boutique hotel that would cost $200 or more in Singapore or Hong Kong.

Bangkok-Specific Booking Tips

Start your Bangkok search on Agoda. The company has deep roots in Asia and often lists Thai hotels for less than Booking.com or Expedia. A room at $35 on Booking.com is often $28 to $30 on Agoda, which adds up over a week.

Book free-cancellation rates and check prices closer to your arrival date. Hotels here change prices often and cut them when rooms look likely to stay empty. A room at $55 two months out can be $38 to $42 two weeks before if the hotel hasn't filled up. The free-cancellation rate gives you the option to rebook at the lower price.

Skip airport hotels unless you land very late or leave very early. The Airport Rail Link runs from Suvarnabhumi to Makkasan (for the MRT) and Phaya Thai (for the BTS) for at most 45 baht (about $1.30) and takes around 30 minutes. Hotels near the airport cost more than similar rooms on the BTS line, and there is nothing to do nearby.

Watch Out for Hotel Airport Transfers

Many cheap guesthouses push their own airport taxi. It almost always costs more than the Airport Rail Link plus the BTS (a couple of dollars in total) or a metered taxi including expressway tolls and the airport surcharge ($12 to $18, more in heavy traffic). Hotel transfers at $25 to $40 are mostly profit for the guesthouse. Use the meter, Grab or the train.

Best Time to Book for Lowest Prices

Bangkok's low season runs from April through June, when the heat and humidity are at their most intense. Hotel prices hit their low for the year: a room that costs $50 in November might be $28 to $35 in May. Everything stays open and the big temples are less crowded. If you can cope with the heat and aren't tied to school holidays, low season is a bargain.

Peak season runs from November through February, when temperatures are more comfortable and both regional and international tourism peaks. Book ahead for this period: the best budget properties fill quickly and prices rise 25 to 40% compared to the same properties in low season.

Songkran, the Thai New Year on April 13 to 15, turns the whole city into a giant water fight and is a lot of fun if you join in. Prices rise during the festival itself, but you can still find rooms. If you're planning an April trip, check whether your dates overlap.

Where to stay, in short

On a tight budget and want to meet people? Banglamphu, around Khao San Road. Want low prices, a station nearby and great local food? On Nut on the BTS Sukhumvit line. Want more comfort for under $60? Silom on a weekend, or a mid-range hotel anywhere near the BTS. And for Bangkok, start your search on Agoda.
