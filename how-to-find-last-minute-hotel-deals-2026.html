How to Find Last-Minute Hotel Deals in 2026: The Strategies That Work and the Apps Worth Using

By Amara Nwosu12 min readHotels
Disclosure: Some links in this guide may earn LitExplore a small commission at no extra cost to you. That never changes what we recommend, and we only link to services we think are useful.

Hotel rooms are perishable inventory: a room that goes unsold tonight generates zero revenue and cannot be stored and sold tomorrow. Faced with a choice between a big discount and an empty room, most hotels take the discount. A room that cost full price two weeks ahead can sometimes go for much less on the day, simply because nobody had booked it by late afternoon.

Hotels cannot advertise this aggressively, because doing so would train every customer to wait until the last minute, destroying the advance-booking revenue they depend on. Instead, they sell leftover rooms quietly through apps like HotelTonight, "mystery hotel" deals on Hotwire and Priceline, and late member rates in their own apps, at prices they'd never advertise weeks ahead.

That's the whole secret behind last-minute deals. They aren't gimmicks. They exist because an empty room earns nothing, and hotels reward guests who are flexible enough to fill it.

Why Hotels Discount at the Last Minute

Hotels set prices using a practice called revenue management, and the basics are simple enough.

Hotels aim to sell rooms at the highest possible rate for each night. Their pricing algorithms adjust rates constantly based on current booking pace, historical data, competitive pricing, and predicted demand. As a night gets closer with rooms still empty, the hotel has to decide whether to hold its price and risk empty rooms or cut the price to fill them. That depends on how many rooms are left and how much time remains.

Most hotels have a 24 to 48 hour cancellation policy. Many guests who have booked in advance cancel within this window when plans change. That frees up a wave of rooms in the last day or two before each night, which the hotel then has to sell fast. So last-minute deals come not only from rooms nobody booked, but also from rooms that were booked and then cancelled.

HotelTonight was built around exactly these late-released rooms and sends them to travelers looking for a deal. Hotwire's Hot Rates work in a similar way, with a twist: you don't learn the hotel's name until after you book, and in exchange the discount can be bigger.

When Last-Minute Booking Works Best

Last-minute deals aren't always there. They work well in some situations and badly in others.

📅

Weekend stays at business hotels in commercial cities

Business hotels in financial centers (London, Frankfurt, New York, Singapore, Dubai) have occupancy patterns driven by corporate travel: high Monday through Thursday, lower Friday and Saturday. Weekend and Sunday-night rates at these hotels are often far below their usual price, because the business crowd has gone home and leisure travelers don't fill the gap. It's one of the most dependable last-minute opportunities in big commercial cities.

🌦️

Shoulder and off-season periods

When destination occupancy is generally low, last-minute availability is high and discounts are deeper. A beach resort in the Caribbean during October or November, or a European city hotel in February, has much more unsold inventory available at the last minute than the same property in peak July or August. If you can travel in the off-season and book late, you'll find the biggest discounts.

🏨

Urban hotels with high room counts

Large hotels with 200 or more rooms have more unsold inventory on any given night and more pressure to fill it than small boutique properties with 20 rooms. Big hotels offer last-minute deals far more often, and bigger ones, than small boutique places. Tiny ryokans, much-loved design hotels and top-rated boutiques often sell out weeks ahead and never discount.

📊

Properties that use yield management actively

Big chains such as Marriott, Hilton, IHG and Hyatt use pricing software that pushes unsold rooms out through discount channels as the date approaches. Independent hotels are less likely to do this and may prefer an empty room to a heavy discount. For last-minute deals, mid-range chain hotels are usually a better bet than independents.

When Last-Minute Booking Will Fail You

Sometimes waiting until the last minute will cost you more, or leave you with nowhere to sleep.

Major events and high-demand weekends. If you are booking last-minute for a night coinciding with a major music festival, a significant sporting event, a national holiday weekend, or a trade show that has booked out a conference hotel block, you may find virtually no availability at any price. Edinburgh in August, the area around Glastonbury Festival, or any city on its Formula 1 weekend can run out of rooms entirely. Waiting isn't a strategy there. It's a gamble that can leave you paying triple or sleeping an hour's drive away.

Small towns and the countryside. A town with four hotels doesn't really have last-minute deals. Empty rooms simply stay empty or go to people the owners know. In rural areas, worry about finding a room at all, not about finding a bargain.

Special or top-rated places. The 12-room ryokan in the hills outside Kyoto with a four-month waiting list won't be on HotelTonight. Places with a great reputation and few rooms don't need to discount, because they fill up early. If a particular property is the point of your trip, book ahead.

Trips where the room itself matters. A last-minute deal means picking from what's left, not from what suits you best. If the neighborhood, type of hotel or room standard matters a lot to you, book ahead so you get your first choice.

The Best Apps and Platforms for Last-Minute Hotel Deals in 2026

Best Overall

HotelTonight

Best for same-day and next-day bookings

An app built for last-minute hotel deals, now owned by Airbnb but still run as its own brand. It sells rooms hotels haven't filled, mostly for tonight and the coming days, though you can also book further ahead. The app is easy to use, with good photos, maps and simple labels for each hotel's style that help you decide quickly.

Its Daily Drop feature unlocks one extra deal a day, which you have a short time to book. When I decide on a trip at the last minute, it's the first app I open. Frequent users get extra discounts through HT Perks. Coverage is best in big cities in North America and Europe, with some in Asia-Pacific.

Best for Opaque Deals

Hotwire Hot Rates

Best for deep discounts with brand flexibility

Hotwire's Hot Rate product shows you the star rating, neighborhood, amenities, and guest review score of a property but conceals the hotel name until after you complete the booking. The discounts can be large, sometimes 40 to 60% off, because hotels can fill rooms without publicly showing the lower price. Read the listing's amenities and review scores closely, since they often narrow down which hotels it could be. You usually can't cancel or change the booking, so it suits travelers who are sure of their night and happy with any good hotel in that area.

Similar to Hotwire

Priceline Express Deals

Best for knowing three possible hotel options

Priceline's Express Deals also hide the specific hotel name until after booking, but their Pricebreakers variation shows you three possible hotels and guarantees you will get one of them. Discounts are often in the 25 to 50% range. If booking completely blind makes you nervous, Pricebreakers is a good compromise, since you know the shortlist. It works best in cities you know, where you'll recognize the hotels.

Best for Near-Term Flexibility

Booking.com (last-minute filter)

Best for 2 to 14 days out with full transparency

Booking.com's last-minute deals filter (available under "Special offers") shows properties offering discounts for stays within the next 14 days. These are not as deeply discounted as HotelTonight or Hotwire same-day rates, but they have the advantage of full transparency: you see exactly which property at exactly what price before committing. For trips a few days to two weeks away, this filter plus Genius discounts (free to join) often turns up good rates that you can still cancel.

Worth Checking

Google Hotels (Last Minute)

Best for a quick overview of prices

Google Hotels isn't a last-minute specialist, but it pulls in prices from many booking sites and hotel websites. Start there to see the range of prices before you check the specialist apps. The date flexibility calendar also shows you whether the night immediately adjacent to your target date might be cheaper, useful for single-night spontaneous trips where you can shift by a day.

Regional Specialist

Agoda (Asia Last Minute)

Best for last-minute in Asia Pacific

As our other hotel guides mention, Agoda is often cheaper than Booking.com in Asia. Its Secret Deals, free for members, can show rooms 15 to 30% below the normal rate for stays that same week in Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, Japan and South Korea. For a last-minute booking in Asia-Pacific, start with Agoda.

Specific Tactics That Work in 2026

Call the hotel for tonight's rate. Few people do this, and it often works. Ring the front desk, not the central reservations line, around 3 or 4 PM on the day. Say you need a room for tonight and ask what rate they can offer if you book directly. Front desk staff often have some freedom on same-day prices, and a direct booking saves the hotel the commission it pays booking sites. A friendly call can get you a rate close to the apps' deals.

Use the hotel's own app for member rates. Many major chain hotels (Marriott Bonvoy, Hilton, Hyatt) release "member rates" through their own apps that are exclusive to loyalty members and not visible on OTAs. These often apply on same-day bookings too. Joining the free program takes two minutes and can show rates 10 to 20% below the booking sites, even at short notice.

Ask later in the day. A hotel that still has empty rooms at 6 PM has only a few hours left to sell them. Asking in the early evening rather than at 2 PM can get you a better offer, because the hotel is running out of time. The risk is that the best-value rooms are already gone, so only do this when there are plenty of hotels nearby.

Be flexible on neighborhood rather than city. When searching HotelTonight or Hotwire, do not lock yourself into a single neighborhood if you have transit mobility. The best deal is often one stop away from where you first looked. An $85 room in one area against a $55 room 15 minutes away by metro is a $30 saving for a short ride, and usually worth it.

Last-Minute vs Advance Booking: When Each Wins

Book in advance if...
You have your heart set on a particular hotel. Your trip falls during a big event or holiday. You're traveling as a family or group and need certain rooms. There aren't many places to stay at your destination. Not having a confirmed room would make you anxious. Or the non-refundable advance rate is much cheaper than the flexible one.
Wait for last-minute if...
You don't mind which hotel, as long as it's good. You're staying over a weekend in a business-focused city. You're traveling in the off-season. Advance prices look high for that city. You enjoy being spontaneous and can live with the odd miss.
Hybrid approach (often best)
Book a free-cancellation rate early as a safety net, then watch the last-minute apps as your date gets closer. If something much cheaper turns up on HotelTonight or with the hotel directly, book it and cancel the original. You get a confirmed room plus the chance of a late bargain. It works especially well for solo travelers on short city trips.

The Risks of Booking Last Minute

The biggest risk is simple: what you want may not be available. Last-minute deals depend on rooms being left over, and there's no guarantee there will be any. At busy times, in popular places and at small hotels, there may be nothing left at any price.

The second risk is quality uncertainty on opaque bookings. Hotwire and Priceline Express Deals show you a star rating and neighborhood but not the specific property. Usually a 4-star hotel in a good area is a perfectly good place to sleep. Now and then it comes with slow WiFi, tired rooms and building work next door. If the room quality matters on a particular trip, a mystery booking is a real gamble.

The third risk is stress. Some people are happy to book a room on the day. Others worry all day without one, however much they might save. Know which kind of traveler you are and book accordingly.

Never last-minute book during major events

If your trip overlaps with a big sporting event, music festival, summit, large trade fair or public holiday, waiting to book is asking for trouble. During major events, a city can run out of rooms weeks ahead. Check whether any significant events overlap with your planned travel dates before deciding to wait for a last-minute rate. A quick Google search for "[city] + [your travel month] + events" takes 30 seconds and can save you from an unpleasant surprise.

The practical last-minute playbook

Need a room tonight in a big city? Open HotelTonight first, since it was built for exactly this. Compare with Google Hotels to see the wider range of prices. If you don't mind which hotel you get, try Hotwire Hot Rates for a deeper discount. If a particular chain suits you, check its app for member rates. And if nothing appeals, call a hotel you like around 4 PM and ask what they can do. On most ordinary weeknights in big cities, these five steps will find you a decent room, often for well below the standard booking-site price.
