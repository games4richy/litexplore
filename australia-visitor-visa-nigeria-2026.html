Australia Visitor Visa (Subclass 600) for Nigerians: The Complete 2026 Application Guide

By Amara Nwosu July 3, 2026 16 min read Visa & Immigration
Disclosure: Some links in this guide may earn LitExplore a small commission at no extra cost to you. That never changes what we recommend, and we only link to services we think are useful.

Australia's Subclass 600 Visitor Visa is well documented and also demanding. For Nigerian applicants, the level of scrutiny sits somewhere between the UK Standard Visitor Visa and the US B1/B2. The Department of Home Affairs looks at three things above all: whether you plan to leave when the visit ends, whether you can pay for the trip, and how firmly you are tied to Nigeria. Get those right and you can have a grant within weeks. Get them wrong and you lose the fee along with months of planning.

This guide walks Nigerian passport holders through the 2026 process: who is eligible, what to upload, how long it takes, what it costs, and the mistakes that lead to refusals.

What the Subclass 600 Visa Covers

The Subclass 600 Visitor Visa allows entry to Australia for tourism, visiting family or friends, or short-term business activities such as attending conferences or negotiating contracts (it does not permit paid work for an Australian employer). For Nigerian applicants, the relevant stream is almost always the Tourist stream, There is also a Sponsored Family stream for people with an eligible relative in Australia who is willing to act as a formal sponsor, which can add weight to an application.

Grants are usually for 3, 6 or 12 months, and you can normally stay up to 3 months per visit unless your grant letter says otherwise. Most first-time Nigerian applicants get 3 months, single or multiple entry. Longer grants tend to go to people who have traveled abroad before and have solid finances and ties at home.

Eligibility Requirements

To qualify, you need:

  • a Nigerian passport valid for at least 6 months beyond your planned stay
  • a real intention to visit and then go home (Home Affairs judges this from your whole application, not from one document)
  • enough money to cover the trip without working
  • health insurance for the visit (not compulsory for everyone, but case officers increasingly expect to see it)
  • no character or health issues serious enough to trigger a refusal

If you are under 75, you usually won't need a medical exam for a standard tourist visit. A case officer can still ask for one, and that happens more often for longer stays or when a health concern shows up. You must declare any criminal record. A conviction you disclose is judged on its facts. One that Home Affairs finds out about later is treated far more harshly.

Proving You Will Return Home

Home Affairs wants to be satisfied that you will go back to Nigeria when your visit ends and are not using a visitor visa as a route to staying long term. There is no form for this. The officer forms a view from everything you submit: your ties to Nigeria, your travel history, your finances, and whether your trip plan makes sense.

Required Documentation

The Subclass 600 application is submitted entirely online through ImmiAccount, Australia's official visa application portal. The core documentation package for Nigerian applicants typically includes:

  • Valid passport with at least 6 months' remaining validity and at least one blank page.
  • Passport-style digital photo meeting Australian visa photo specifications.
  • Evidence of financial capacity: six months of bank statements showing a steady balance that covers the trip. No official minimum is published. For a 2 to 4 week visit, AUD $5,000 to $10,000 in funds you can explain is a sensible target.
  • Evidence of employment or income: employment letter confirming your position, salary, and approved leave dates, or business registration and tax documents if self-employed.
  • Travel itinerary: an outline of where you will go and what you will do. You don't need non-refundable bookings.
  • Evidence of ties to Nigeria: property, family responsibilities, a job, or a business that gives you a clear reason to come home.
  • Travel history: copies of previous visas and stamps. Past trips to the UK, the Schengen Area or the US carry the most weight.
  • Health insurance for the length of the visit. It is not always required, but include it if you can, and explain in your cover letter if you can't.
  • Invitation letter (if visiting family or friends), including the host's Australian visa or citizenship status, address, and a description of the relationship.

Processing times move up and down with demand. Home Affairs publishes them as ranges, not promises, so check the current figure on its website before you fix travel dates. Apply 8 to 12 weeks ahead, and leave even more time if you are traveling during Australia's busy December to February season.

Biometrics and the Application Process

Most Nigerian applicants have to give fingerprints and a photo at a Visa Application Centre run by VFS Global, with centers in Lagos and Abuja. You will be asked to book this after you submit the online form. Book straight away, because appointments can be scarce in busy months.

Everything else happens in ImmiAccount. You create an account, fill in the form, upload your documents, pay, and then attend biometrics. Take care with the form. When your answers don't match your documents, officers often refuse the application or ask for more information. Unlike the US B1/B2, most Subclass 600 applicants never have an interview, though a case officer may email you with questions.

Why Nigerian Applications Get Refused

Home Affairs does not publish a separate refusal rate for Nigerian applicants. Immigration lawyers and consultants who handle these cases, though, tend to name the same few problems again and again.

Insufficient or unclear evidence of financial capacity. A big deposit that lands just before you apply, with nothing to show where it came from, is a well-known red flag. Case officers look for it. A balance that has grown slowly over months is far more convincing.

Weak or ambiguous ties to Nigeria. If you are young, single, between jobs or own little in Nigeria, you will find this part harder. That is not bias. There is simply less on paper showing why you would come back. People in this position still get visas, but the rest of the application has to do more work: a clear reason for the trip, solid finances and a sensible itinerary.

Inconsistencies between the application and supporting documents. Mismatched dates, employment details that don't match the employment letter, or an itinerary that doesn't align with the stated purpose of travel make an officer doubt the whole application, even if every document is real.

Incomplete documentation. Missing bank statements, an employment letter without key details (salary, approved leave, position, company letterhead and contact details), or a photo that doesn't meet the rules can lead to a request for more information, which adds weeks. If the gaps are serious, you may simply be refused.

If You've Been Refused Before

A refusal does not ban you from applying again. Your next application, though, must deal head-on with the reason given in the refusal letter, which usually names a concern such as finances, ties, or doubts about your intentions. Sending the same documents again and hoping for a friendlier officer rarely works.

How to Strengthen a Nigerian Application

Consultants who handle Nigerian applications point to a few steps that make a real difference.

Start building your bank history early. Three to six months of ordinary, steady balances beats a large sum that showed up two weeks before you applied. If you are self-employed, register the business with CAC if you haven't, and keep records tidy enough that an officer who knows nothing about Nigerian trading can follow them.

Write a cover letter that sets out why you are going, what you will do, when you will return, and what brings you back: your job, family, property or other commitments. Don't count on the documents to tell the story alone. Officers work through piles of files, and when something is unclear they rarely give you the benefit of the doubt.

If you have traveled before, especially to countries with tough visa rules, put that evidence up front. A record of keeping to visa conditions and coming home on time is one of the best signs that you will do the same in Australia.

The Sponsored Family Stream

If you have an eligible relative in Australia (a parent, child or sibling, and in some cases another relative or partner), you can apply under the Sponsored Family stream instead of the Tourist stream. The sponsor must be an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen. They fill in a separate sponsorship form and take formal responsibility for parts of your visit. Home Affairs may also ask them for a security bond.

A well-documented sponsorship helps. Someone in Australia is formally vouching for you, and they often add money to your own. It does not decide the case, though. Your ties to Nigeria, your intentions and your own documents still count, and a strong sponsor cannot fully rescue a weak application.

Sponsors need to provide evidence of their own status in Australia (citizenship or residency documents), evidence of the relationship to the applicant (birth certificates, marriage certificates, or other relationship evidence depending on the connection), and in some cases financial documentation if the sponsorship includes a financial support component. The sponsorship form is submitted separately through ImmiAccount and linked to the applicant's visa application.

Writing an Effective Cover Letter

A cover letter is optional, but almost every consultant recommends one. It lets you explain your situation in your own words so the officer isn't left to guess your intentions from a stack of documents.

Make it personal to you. State the purpose of the visit (a wedding, a set tourist route, a named conference), your dates, and who is traveling with you. Then spell out your ties to Nigeria: your job and how long you have held it, your family responsibilities, any property or business, and why you must be back by your return date, whether that is work, a school term or a family commitment.

Skip template phrases that could come from anyone. "I intend to visit Australia as a tourist and will return to Nigeria after my visit" tells the officer nothing new. A better letter names the places or relatives you will visit, points to the attached evidence (bank statements, employment letter), and explains anything that might raise an eyebrow, such as a recent job change, a gap in your travel history, or a large deposit and where it came from.

Common Mistakes on the ImmiAccount Application

Some strong applications get delayed by simple admin errors. The most common is a mismatch in personal details, such as a name spelled one way on the form and another way on a document, or a mistyped date of birth or passport number. Usually it is just a typo, but it can still prompt a query that costs you weeks.

Poor scans cause trouble too: blurry bank statements, employment letters with the letterhead or signature cut off, photos with the wrong size or background. These usually lead to a request for better copies rather than a refusal, but the delay is real. Read the official Subclass 600 requirements page yourself before you submit instead of relying on what friends or agents tell you. It will catch most of these problems.

The easiest mistake to avoid is applying too late. Standard processing takes 20 to 45 days, and a request for more information can add weeks. If you have already paid for a non-refundable flight three weeks out, one query from the case officer can wreck the trip.

After Approval: What the Visa Allows

Your visa is linked to your passport electronically, so there is no sticker to collect. Read the grant notice closely. It sets out how long the visa is valid, whether you get one entry or several, and how long each stay can last. These details differ from person to person, even within the same subclass.

You cannot do paid work of any kind on this visa, including casual or short jobs. Doing so breaches your visa conditions and will count against you in future applications. Business visitors can attend meetings, conferences and negotiations. They cannot do hands-on work for an Australian company, and some business travelers get caught out by that line.

Applying as a Family or Group

Every family member, children included, needs their own application and fee. There is no household application. You can lodge the applications at the same time, and they are often handled by one case officer, which helps when the paperwork is tidy and tells one consistent story. Children need a birth certificate. If a child travels with one parent or with another adult, include a consent letter from the parent who is staying behind. Officers often ask for it anyway.

Money is usually assessed for the household as a whole. Each person does not need to show the full amount, as long as the main applicant's documents clearly cover the whole group. Say so in the cover letter so the officer doesn't have to work it out.

Extending Your Stay in Australia

If your plans change and you want to stay longer, you can apply for a new Subclass 600 from inside Australia before your current visa runs out. You will need to meet the requirements again, including funds and temporary intent. This is a new application, not an automatic extension, and officers check onshore applications for signs that you never meant to leave.

Lodge the new application before your current visa expires and you will normally get a bridging visa, which lets you stay legally while you wait, even past your original expiry date. Staying without a valid visa, even for a few days, is treated seriously and can hurt future applications. Leave plenty of margin so there is never a gap.

A Realistic Application Timeline

Take an applicant with a stable job, clear ties to Nigeria and a decent savings record. Their timeline usually runs like this. Two to four weeks collecting bank statements and work documents and writing the cover letter. One day to submit and pay. One to two weeks to get and attend a biometrics slot in Lagos or Abuja. Then two to six weeks of processing, sometimes more if the officer asks for extra documents.

Plan on eight to twelve weeks from the day you start gathering papers to the day you hold a grant. Wait until you have the visa before paying for non-refundable flights or hotels. It will save you a lot of worry.

Is the Subclass 600 Process Worth the Effort?

If you have a real reason to go, whether that is tourism, family or business, then yes. The process is demanding, but the rules are published and processing is usually quicker and more predictable than for several other Western visitor visas. The people who struggle tend to treat the application as a form to fill in. It works better when you treat it as a case you have to build and argue, with your finances and your ties at its center.

Many Nigerians apply for Schengen or UK visas around the same time. The documents are much alike across all three: proof of funds, ties to Nigeria and a clear reason for the trip. What sets Australia apart is the format. Most applicants have no interview, the whole process is online, and processing times are published more clearly than for some other visas. That doesn't make the assessment any easier, but it does make the process easier to plan around.

Three habits separate the approvals from the refusals, even between people in similar circumstances. Apply early. Build your bank history over months, not weeks. And write a clear cover letter that explains why you will come home. You don't need to pay an agent a big fee for any of this. It takes time, organization, and a truthful, well-evidenced account of your plans.
